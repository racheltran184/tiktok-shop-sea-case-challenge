Three markets, three ways to take GMV apart

I took on all three country cases: a single-creator livestream in Vietnam, a wallet-share plan in Malaysia and an 11.11 creator plan in Thailand. Each time I broke the GMV target into its drivers, found the one that had to move, and built the tool or prototype that makes the plan runnable.

Vietnam, 12.12: one creator, one day, US$1M

Project: Trust in Every Click

The brief

Take one emerging creator to US$1M GMV in a single 12.12 livestream, through strategy and operations rather than aggressive subsidy.

What the numbers said

Only Creator B can reach US$1M inside a day: 14.8 hours at B’s peak of US$67.8K an hour, against 30 to 73 hours for the other four. With conversion and order value held at B’s peak, the whole gap is views: +74%, closed by a 15-hour live at about 98% of B’s peak views per hour.

What we proposed

A 63-day runway in five phases, each with its biggest risk, an early-warning signal and an owner, and a go/no-go gate on 14 November: B keeps the slot only if the 11.11 rehearsal sells at least US$68K an hour; otherwise Creator E, on standby since day one, takes it.

What I built

Tích Tắc 12.12 Mission Control. Each hour the ops team enters cumulative GMV, orders and views. The tool tests the hour against one fixed bar of US$66,667 (US$1M ÷ 15 hours), shows Watch after one miss and Act now after two, names the weakest of the three drivers and shows the pre-agreed move and its owner. It imports and exports Excel and treats missing hours as unknown, never as zero.

Malaysia: help a parent place a safe first order

Project: Order Pertama Mak

The brief

Win a larger share of Malaysian online spending in 12 months, with headcount for only one segment pilot and no blanket discounts.

What the numbers said

Growth isn’t a reach problem. Only 34% of product viewers add to cart and 42% of checkouts never finish. Campaign-led buyers cost RM163 per repeat customer against RM31 for content-led, yet take 55% of acquisition spend. Shoppers aged 41+ have the biggest basket (RM39) and the fewest orders (44 a year): closing half the gap to 31–40 year olds is worth about RM174M a year.

What we proposed

Reach parents through their children. An 18–25 year old sends one product card with the total price, delivery date and protections; the parent decides, and either of them can pay. A three-arm test of about 50,000 users per arm decides the rest: scale at 8 or more parent first orders per 100 young shoppers in the test, 45% repeat and RM20 cost or less; stop below 5, below 40% or above RM31.

What I built

The two-phone journey prototype: Aina’s phone and Mak’s side by side, eighteen steps across two paths, Mak Mode in Malay, English, Chinese and Tamil, and a 24-hour price lock in place of a new discount.

Thailand, 11.11: back 300 mid-tier creators on the top’s terms

Project: Rising Stars 300

The brief

Hit +50% GMV against the last mega campaign when top creators are half sold out four weeks ahead and most small sellers can’t get a slot.

What the numbers said

Traffic sells hardest at the top, 1.25 GMV per traffic point, but mid-tier traffic still sells at 1.00 against 0.60 in the long tail, and 70% of mid creators have no campaign deal. Meanwhile 80% of small sellers can’t book a top creator and hold about 30% of their budget unallocated.

What we proposed

Keep the top’s half of the budget, and give the best 300 mid creators, ranked by GMV per traffic point from the half who go live on time, the top’s terms: about 17× today’s budget per creator, the same feed priority and a 1:1 co-funded deal. If each sells half what a top creator does, 11.11 reaches 150.

What I built

The brand-journey prototype, in which a stuck seller meets a Rising Star in their own For You feed, books in one flow and gets one follow-up if they leave halfway, plus the scorecards that judge each journey step and the 300 creators like a sales team.

What carries over to an e‑commerce team

  • Break the target down before choosing a lever

    Every brief here split into a few multiplied or added drivers, and in each only one had to move.

  • Set the bar before the event

    Pass marks, gates and stop rules were fixed in advance, so on the day the data decides and nobody argues about thresholds.

  • Label every number

    Case data, our arithmetic and our assumptions are tagged separately in every deck, tool and notebook cell.

  • Ship something people can use

    Each plan came with a working tool or clickable journey, not only slides.
