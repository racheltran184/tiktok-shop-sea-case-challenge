01 · The method

Four steps from
mandate to decision.

Leadership does not buy analysis; it buys a decision it can defend. So every mandate went through the same commercial sequence, and each step answers the question a stakeholder would ask in the room.

The question each step answers

1 · Identify the problemWhich driver of the target is broken: traffic, conversion, basket, frequency or supply?
2 · Find the pain pointWho hesitates at that step, and what are they worried about?
3 · Show the solutionWhat do we change with levers the platform already has, before spending on discounts?
4 · The operationsWho does what by when, what is the warning signal, and when do we stop?

Who did what

  • VietnamOperations project lead in team The Three of Cups, with Nguyen Khuat Son Tra (strategy lead) and Nguyen Do Minh Huu (campaign lead). I owned the 63-day runway, the risk gates and the live-day Mission Control. The deck lists me as Tran Ngoc Bao Han.
  • Malaysia & ThailandTeam The Fools, a duo with Audrey Nguyen, who led strategy, story and video. I owned the data, prototypes and metrics: the unit economics behind each recommendation, the scorecards and decision gates, and the prototypes stakeholders can click through.
  • EvidenceAll case figures are fictional, supplied by the organisers. Every proposal separates supplied evidence from our own calculations and ends in a decision a stakeholder can approve, defer or stop.

The one lever in each case

In each case the mandate reduces to one lever a business leader can pull: the underlined term. Everything else in the equation is held steady with operational guardrails, not more spending.

02 · Vietnam · 12.12 campaign

One creator, one day,
US$1M.

Project: Trust in Every ClickTeam: The Three of CupsMy role: operations lead

“Earn the trust, earn the US$1M. Don’t buy it.”

The ask

Back Creator B for a 15-hour, shift-run 12.12 live, commit to a 63-day runway, and lock the slot only if the 11.11 rehearsal sells at least US$68K an hour.

Runway in five gated stages
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Find a creator who can sell US$1M inside one day, without buying it with subsidy.

    Management wanted one emerging creator to deliver US$1M GMV in a single 12.12 livestream without margin-eroding platform subsidies. Of the five candidates, only Creator B sells fast enough to finish inside the campaign day: US$67.8K an hour, so 14.8 hours to US$1M, against 30 to 73 hours for the other four.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    B's best live ran 8.5 hours. The plan needs 15, with 74% more views.

    Creator B's peak session ran 8.5 hours and sold US$0.58M. Reaching US$1M means +74% more views across a 15-hour broadcast while holding conversion (1.27 orders per 1,000 views) and basket (US$10.30). Over a marathon, two frictions kill GMV: host fatigue and traffic decay in off-peak hours, and shopper hesitation, since 67% of online shoppers surveyed want a guarantee that a product is authentic before they buy.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Earn trust on camera instead of cutting prices.

    A customer-centric 4Cs live funnel built around B's proven mix of Health & Beauty (60%) and Fashion (40%). Official brand stock and live shade and size consultations build trust; timed, brand-funded drops hold viewers through the day; and a US$12–13 free-shipping threshold is the only platform-capped lever, used to lift basket size.

  4. 04

    The operations

    A runway with an owner, a warning signal and a stop rule at every stage.

    Five stages from signed commitment to live day, each with an early-warning signal; Creator E briefed on standby from day one; and a hard 11.11 rehearsal gate of at least US$68K an hour to lock the slot. For the day itself I designed Tích Tắc 12.12 Mission Control: ops logs hourly GMV, orders and views against a fixed US$66,667 pass mark, flags Watch after one miss, and after two triggers a pre-agreed move (ad top-up, co-host swap or pace reset), never an unplanned subsidy.

03 · Malaysia · 12-month wallet share

Help a parent place
a safe first order.

Project: Order Pertama MakTeam: The FoolsMy role: data, prototypes, metrics

“Help a parent make a safe first purchase, then earn the second.”

The ask

Approve an 8-week, three-arm pilot in West Malaysia with no new discounts, and agree the rule now: scale at month 7 only with 8 or more parent first orders per 100 Scouts, 45% 90-day repeat and a cost of RM20 or less per order.

5×

0
New discounts: the same promotions run in every arm
  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    Growth is leaking through retention and inefficient acquisition spend.

    TikTok Shop Malaysia grew GMV 30% a year, but campaign-led acquisition takes 55% of the acquisition budget and costs RM163 per repeat buyer, five times the RM31 of content-led acquisition. With headcount for only one segment pilot, blanket discounting was off the table. We targeted shoppers aged 41+ (“Anchors”): the biggest basket (RM39) and the fewest orders (44 a year).

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    Awareness is not the bottleneck; trust at consideration and checkout is.

    Across the platform, 66% of product viewers never add to cart and 42% of started checkouts are abandoned. When 18–25 year olds (“Scouts”) spot a deal and forward a screenshot to a parent on a chat app, the parent stalls on three unanswered questions: Is it authentic? When will it arrive? Can I return it?

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Turn children into the trust channel for their parents.

    Order Pertama Mak: instead of screenshots or new subsidies, a young shopper sends one verified Jemput Mak product card showing the existing Mall authenticity guarantee, one delivery date and free 30-day returns in the parent's language, backed by a shared family cart (Troli Keluarga) and a 24-hour price lock (Kunci Deal). Closing half the frequency gap to 31–40 year olds is worth RM174M a year in GMV and saves RM16–33M in acquisition spend.

  4. 04

    The operations

    Let a randomised test decide, with the stop rule agreed up front.

    An 8-week, three-arm randomised trial in West Malaysia, 50,000 Scouts per arm, with zero added discounts, plus an interactive two-phone journey so product and ops teams see the same experience. Pre-committed gates govern the month-7 capital decision, with PDPA consent and payment caps enforced throughout.

  1. 01

    Identify the problem

    +50% GMV, with the top of the market already sold out.

    Thailand leadership targeted +50% GMV for 11.11, but creator supply was gridlocked at the top. The top 100 creators generate 50% of live GMV at 1.25 GMV per traffic point, yet half their slots are pre-bought by large anchor brands four weeks out, and anchor brands warned they would pull budget if top-creator traffic was cut. With the top capped at 50, the rest of the marketplace had to double, from 50 to 100.

  2. 02

    Find the pain point

    Idle budget on one side, idle creators on the other.

    On the demand side, 80% of SMB sellers can't get a top slot and have no fallback, leaving 30% of their campaign budgets unallocated. On the supply side, mid-tier creators convert traffic far better than the long tail (1.00 against 0.60 GMV per traffic point), yet 70% have no deal and only 50% start their lives on time, so sellers are afraid to risk budget on them.

  3. 03

    Show the solution

    Sell the middle to brands as a vetted product: Rising Stars 300.

    We protected the top 100's 50% budget share in writing and reframed the mid-tier as a vetted B2B marketplace product. From 900 mid creators we keep the 450 who go live on time, pick the top 300 by GMV per traffic point, and concentrate 25% of platform budget on them (~17× their previous per-creator support). Matched with idle SMB budgets through 1:1 co-funding and top-tier feed priority, each Rising Star only needs to sell half what a top creator does for 11.11 to reach 150.

  4. 04

    The operations

    No new build before 11.11: nine tools sellers already use, and one checkpoint.

    A push-based seller journey across nine existing Seller Center tools. Unmatched SMBs see a matched Rising Star clip in their own For You feed, unlock one-tap booking after 3 seconds, and only pay co-funding if the live starts on time, backed by automatic standby swaps (ranks 301–450) within 10 minutes of a no-show and an executive scorecard that checks for 85% of slots booked on 4 November before scaling into 12.12.

05 · Questions in the room

Pressure-test the plan
before the board does.

A pitch that only works if everything goes right is a forecast, not a plan. After submission I rebuilt the numbers of all three decks in one notebook and stress-tested the single assumption each proposal leans on hardest, so each plan comes with its own guardrails, not just its upside.

Malaysia · what if fewer parents activate?

Year-1 GMV ranges RM80–160M across 5–10% activation and 50–100% capture, and even the weakest case clears RM80M. The scenario is illustrative, so the month-7 gate, not the forecast, decides whether to scale.

How I turn a commercial problem into a plan a board can approve

1 · Diagnose the commercial bottleneck

Start from the headline target and break it into its P&L and funnel drivers, separating reach problems from conversion, retention or marketplace allocation gaps.

2 · Isolate the human pain point

Trace every leaking metric to the moment a shopper, parent or SMB seller hesitates, whether over authenticity, checkout friction or creator reliability.

3 · Design subsidy-disciplined solutions

Solve the friction with better marketplace matching, existing platform guarantees and cart thresholds, not blanket discounts that only buy this quarter's GMV.

4 · Build governance a stakeholder can run

Hand leadership a runnable plan: dated milestones, standby contingencies, pre-agreed stop rules and a live decision dashboard, so the board has a clear go/no-go, not a forecast to take on faith.
