Vietnam, 12.12: one creator, one day, US$1M

Project: Trust in Every Click

1. Identify the Problem

Management wanted one emerging creator to deliver US$1M GMV in a single 12.12 livestream without relying on margin-eroding platform subsidies. Evaluating the five candidates showed only Creator B had the hourly sales velocity (US$67.8K an hour) to finish inside a 24-hour campaign day — needing 14.8 hours against 30 to 73 hours for the other four.

2. Find the Pain Point

Creator B’s peak session only ran for 8.5 hours (US$0.58M). Scaling to US$1M required +74% more views across a 15-hour broadcast while holding conversion (1.27) and AOV (US$10.30) steady. Across a 15-hour marathon, two frictions kill GMV: host fatigue and traffic decay in off-peak hours, and shopper hesitation when 67% of Vietnamese buyers demand proof of product authenticity before checking out.

3. Show the Solution

We designed a customer-centric 4Cs live funnel around Creator B’s core Health & Beauty (60%) and Fashion (40%) mix. Rather than cutting prices across the board, we paired official brand stock and live shade/size consultations to build trust with timed brand-funded drops to hold viewership, using a US$12–13 free-shipping threshold as the only platform-capped lever to lift basket size.

4. The Operations

We mapped a 63-day, five-stage rollout with early-warning signals, Creator E briefed on standby from day one, and a hard 11.11 rehearsal gate requiring at least US$68K an hour to lock the slot. For live-day governance, I designed Tích Tắc 12.12 Mission Control: ops logs hourly GMV, orders and views against a fixed US$66,667/hour pass mark, flagging Watch after one miss and triggering pre-agreed interventions (ad top-ups, co-host swaps, or pace resets — never unplanned subsidies) after two.

Malaysia: help a parent place a safe first order

Project: Order Pertama Mak

1. Identify the Problem

TikTok Shop Malaysia grew GMV +30% YoY, but growth was leaking through poor retention and inefficient subsidy spend. Campaign-led acquisition consumed 55% of acquisition budget at RM163 per repeat buyer — five times the cost of content-led acquisition (RM31). With headcount for only one segment pilot, blanket discounting was off the table; we targeted 41+ shoppers (“Anchors”), who carry the highest basket (RM39) but the lowest frequency (44 orders a year).

2. Find the Pain Point

Awareness wasn't the bottleneck — trust at consideration and checkout was. Across the platform, 66% of product viewers never add to cart and 42% of initiated checkouts are abandoned. When 18–25 year olds (“Scouts”) spot deals on TikTok and forward screenshots to their parents on chat apps, parents stall over three unanswered questions: Is it authentic? When will it arrive? Can I return it?

3. Show the Solution

We turned 18–25 year olds into an organic onboarding and trust channel for their parents via Order Pertama Mak. Instead of messy screenshots or new subsidies, a young shopper shares one verified Jemput Mak product card stating existing Mall authenticity guarantees, a single delivery date and free 30-day returns in the parent’s preferred language, backed by a shared family cart (Troli Keluarga) and a 24-hour price lock (Kunci Deal). Closing half the frequency gap to 31–40 year olds unlocks RM174M a year in GMV and saves RM16–33M in acquisition spend.

4. The Operations

To give leadership a low-risk decision path, we structured an 8-week, three-arm randomized trial (50,000 Scouts per arm) in West Malaysia with zero added discounts, supported by an interactive two-phone journey to align product and ops teams. Pre-committed executive gates govern capital allocation at Month 7: scale only if the pilot delivers ≥8 parent first orders per 100 Scouts, ≥45% 90-day repeat and <RM20 cost per order while maintaining strict PDPA consent and payment caps.

Thailand, 11.11: back 300 mid-tier creators on the top’s terms

Project: Rising Stars 300

1. Identify the Problem

Thailand leadership targeted +50% GMV for 11.11, but creator supply was gridlocked at the top. The Top 100 creators generate 50% of live GMV at 1.25 GMV per traffic point, yet half their slots are pre-bought by large anchor brands four weeks out — and anchor brands warned they would pull budget if top-creator traffic was cut. To hit 150 with the top capped at 50, the rest of the marketplace had to double from 50 to 100.

2. Find the Pain Point

Both sides of the middle market were stuck. On the demand side, 80% of SMB sellers were locked out of top slots with no fallback matching path, leaving 30% of their campaign budgets unallocated. On the supply side, mid-tier creators convert traffic far better than the long tail (1.00 vs. 0.60 GMV per traffic point), yet 70% had no deal and only 50% started lives on time — making sellers afraid to risk budget on them.

3. Show the Solution

We protected the Top 100’s 50% budget share in writing and reframed the mid-tier as a vetted B2B marketplace product: Rising Stars 300. From 900 mid creators, we filter for the 450 who go live on time, select the top 300 by GMV per traffic point, and concentrate 25% of total platform budget on them (~17× their previous per-creator support). Matched with idle SMB budgets via 1:1 co-funding and top-tier feed priority, each Rising Star only needs to sell half what a top creator does for 11.11 to reach 150.

4. The Operations

Rather than building new infrastructure before 11.11, we embedded a push-based seller journey across nine existing Seller Center tools. Unmatched SMBs see a matched Rising Star clip in their own For You feed, unlock one-tap booking after 3 seconds, and only pay co-funding if the live happens on time — backed by automated standby swaps (ranks 301–450) within 10 minutes of a no-show and an executive scorecard that checks for 85% slot bookings on 4 November before scaling into 12.12.

Preparing for the questions in the room

A pitch that only works if everything goes right is a forecast, not a plan. Before any deck went in front of a board or a judging panel, I consolidated all three financial models into one commercial audit — tracing every exhibit figure and stress-testing the single operational risk each strategy leans on hardest. Each plan arrived with its own guardrails, not just its own upside.

How I turn a commercial problem into a plan a board can approve

  • 1. Diagnose the commercial bottleneck

    Start from the headline target and decompose it into its P&L and funnel drivers first — separating reach problems from conversion, retention, or marketplace allocation gaps.

  • 2. Isolate the human pain point

    Trace every metric leak to the exact moment a shopper, parent, or SMB seller hesitates — whether over product authenticity, checkout friction, or creator reliability.

  • 3. Design subsidy-disciplined solutions

    Solve the friction using better marketplace matching, existing platform guarantees, and cart thresholds — not margin-eroding blanket discounts that only buy this quarter's GMV.

  • 4. Build governance a stakeholder can run

    Hand leadership a runnable plan: dated milestones, standby contingencies, pre-agreed stop rules, and a live decision dashboard — so the board has a clear go/no-go, not a forecast to take on faith.
